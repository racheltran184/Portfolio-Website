Melbourne Airbnb Price Model

Built a price model for 7,000 Melbourne Airbnb listings that placed 1st of 105 teams in a Kaggle competition, on a five-stage, leakage-safe pipeline that turns 61 raw fields into 175 features. A post-competition check traced most of the error to the yardstick, and a fair-price model showed what the gap between asking and fair price means for hosts, guests, platforms and policy makers.

🛠 Tools
Python pandas scikit-learn XGBoost LightGBM Optuna SHAP
⚙️ Techniques
Leakage-Safe Pipeline SHAP Feature Selection Bayesian Hyperparameter Tuning Error Analysis Hedonic Regression
📊 Results
Competition Result: 1st of 105 teams on the private leaderboard with tuned XGBoost (MAE $108.80), 8% lower error than Random Forest and $0.88 ahead of LightGBM on the same features.
Error Audit: Traced the jump from $51 in cross-validation to $109 on the leaderboard to the yardstick: validation scored capped prices, and 10 placeholder prices above $5,000 (0.14% of listings) accounted for 51% of all error.
Location: Found a U-shaped price curve, not a simple distance discount: against the middle ring, central listings ask 17% more and the outer suburbs 9% less, while the Yarra Ranges asks 28% more.
Trust & Amenities: Superhosts charge 5.5% less than comparable listings yet log 22 reviews a year against 7; a fireplace, allowing pets and a dryer add 18%, 14% and 11%, while pools and balconies show no clear premium.
Fair Price: 23% of listings ask 20% or more above their fair price and average 12.9 open nights in the next 30 days against 10.0, while 235 booked-solid listings sit a median $44 a night below it.

🛠 Tools
Power BI DAX Python scikit-learn
⚙️ Techniques
Snowflake Schema Retention Measurement Early-Warning Signals Point-in-Time Modelling

Model Evaluation: Showed why the group model's 93.4% accuracy was the wrong yardstick when predicting "stays" for everyone already scores 89.7%, then built a point-in-time model that catches 70% of leavers when advisers can contact 22% of students.

Urban Eats Café Analytics

Designed an 11-table MySQL database for a three-outlet café chain, audited its records, then took five board questions down to the clock hour, the ticket and the plate. The labour leak is in the roster, not the clock, and the 90-day plan gives every action an owner, a trial and a stop rule.

🛠 Tools
SQL MySQL 8 ERD (Chen notation)
⚙️ Techniques
Schema Design Data Quality Audit CTEs & Window Functions Contribution Analysis Basket Analysis
📊 Results

Labour Leak: From 8 pm the outlets carry 13% of paid hours and none of the 30 orders, while attendance runs only 0.7% over the roster; closing two hours after the last booking frees $280–420 in wages a night.
Takeaway Ticket: 6 of 9 takeaway food orders left without a drink, against 2 of 11 dine-in; every 10 drinks added is $47 of sales and $33 of margin.
Menu Pricing: Mains earn $7.70–13.70 a plate; a $1 test on the three 40% food-cost mains pays off unless their sales fall by more than 6.8–8.1%.
Guests & Bookings: Every repeat customer came back within 15 days (median 11), so the win-back offer goes out on day 10; parties of five or more hold 45% of booked seats.

Victoria Road Safety Analytics

Analysed 13 years of Victorian police crash records (184,699 crashes, 3,318 deaths) in Tableau, R and Python, measuring every question in deaths rather than crash counts. Crashes peak in Melbourne's Friday rush, but deaths concentrate on regional highways, at night, in old cars and among people without a seatbelt, so each of eleven actions starts where people die.

🛠 Tools
Tableau R ggplot2 Python pandas
⚙️ Techniques
Harm-Weighted Analysis Crash-Type Classification Geospatial Mapping Target Gap Tracking Controlled Comparisons
📊 Results
Target Gap: Deaths reached 295 in 2023 and 284 in 2024 while crashes held flat, ending 2024 100 deaths above a straight line to halving road deaths by 2030.
Where People Die: Vehicles leaving their lane on undivided regional 100–110 km/h roads produced 24% of deaths from 5.4% of crashes, and twelve highways carry 23% of regional deaths.
When: The busiest hours aren't the deadliest: 10 pm to 6 am holds 10% of crashes but 20% of deaths, and midnight to 4 am kills 4.2–4.7 people per 100 crashes.
Who: Occupants without a seatbelt died at 9 times the belted rate; motorcyclists are 6% of people in crashes and 17% of deaths, and 6.7% of pedestrians aged 70+ who are hit die.
Old Cars: Driver deaths per 1,000 rise from 2.9 in cars under 5 years old to 13.0 at 20+, and the gap holds on the same roads, at the same driver age and with a seatbelt on.

LuminaTech Sales Briefing

Cleaned 1.09 million ERP sales lines for a lighting supplier in Australia and New Zealand, then audited the data before briefing the leadership team. Three blind spots (a missing half-year, $1.00 placeholder costs and intercompany sales mixed in with customers) turned a reported collapse into flat sales and a 66% margin into 43%, leading to a 90-day plan with an owner and a measure for every action.

🛠 Tools
Python pandas NumPy SciPy statsmodels matplotlib
⚙️ Techniques
ETL Pipeline Rule-Based Imputation Data Quality Audit Welch's t-tests OLS Regression (HC3)
📊 Results
Data Audit: Showed the reported 99% revenue collapse was a gap in the extract: 7 of 10 company codes never report July to December, and sales are flat at $244M to $257M a year.
True Margin: Found $1.00 placeholder costs on 52% of lines and intercompany sales mixed in with customers; the real external margin is 42.6%, not the 66% the raw file shows.
Customer Value: The most frequent fifth of customers bring 73% of external sales, and 10% more orders from the top two fifths would add about $18M a year.
Pricing & Credits: The highest-volume tenth of products carries 57% of sales at a median $6.34 a unit, while $2.75M a year goes out in price-only credits with no goods returned.
Corrected Calls: Project orders carry no margin premium (43.8% against 44.1%) and Brisbane sits mid-pack at 42.2%, overturning two of the original recommendations.
